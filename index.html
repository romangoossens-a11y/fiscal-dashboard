How to read: balance needed = (r − g) / (1 + g) × debt / 100, with debt at the end of last year. Fiscal gap = primary balance minus balance needed: positive means the debt ratio falls. Breakeven yield is the 10Y yield at which the debt ratio is stable, and the yield cushion is how far the actual yield is below it. Main driver tags: yields market, growth, deflator and fiscal IMF forecasts.

Gap at net interest rate: the same calculation with the net interest rate on the whole debt stock (IMF interest paid minus interest received, divided by last year's debt) instead of the 10Y yield. The headline gap shows where debt dynamics are heading as debt is refinanced at market rates, this one shows today's arithmetic. The difference is the refinancing pressure still to come. Because interest received is netted off, the rate is low where governments hold large financial assets.

How the IMF Changed Its View

Country Change+ = better Change− = better IMF outlook
Debt is compared on its projected rise rather than its level, so statistical rebases do not read as a better outlook. Improving: stronger primary balance or flatter debt path. Deteriorating: the opposite. Mixed: one of each. Changes under 0.2 pp (balance) or 1 pp (debt) count as unchanged. Hover a verdict for the reason.
