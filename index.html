Fiscal Gap = pb − pb*, where pb* = (r − g) × dt−1. A positive gap means the primary balance is enough to keep the debt ratio stable. The breakeven yield is the 10Y yield at which the debt ratio is stable, so the gap equals the headroom to it times debt. What Moved the Fiscal Gap

Market rates Real growth Inflation Fiscal stance (pb, debt) Net change

Market rates is the effect of the change in the 10Y yield. Real growth and inflation come from IMF revisions and, over 1Y, the roll to a new year of fundamentals. Higher inflation raises nominal growth, so it flatters the gap without being good news. Contributions sum exactly to the net change. Edits and the yield shift flow through.

Debt is compared on its projected change, not its level, so rebases do not read as better dynamics. Improving means a higher primary balance or a flatter debt path, deteriorating the opposite, mixed both. Revisions under 0.2 pp (balance) or 1 pp (debt) count as unchanged.
